The city should ban most private cars from downtown within five years to reduce
air pollution. Buses and the subway make the policy feasible, and other cities
show that it can work. Most people support cleaner air.

The proposal would exempt emergency vehicles and disabled residents. Residents
of neighborhoods with poor transit, night-shift workers, and low-income
residents may be harmed. A transit-pass subsidy is intended to address the
equity concern, but the draft does not establish whether the subsidy is
sufficient. The boundary of "downtown," the practical scope of the ban, the
comparability of other cities, and the interaction between a broad ban and its
exceptions remain unresolved.
